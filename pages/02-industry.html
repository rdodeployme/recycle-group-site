Redundant material isn't rubbish.

Every working site accumulates it. Plant that has been replaced, steel left over from an upgrade, timber stacked behind a shed, concrete from a job that finished two years ago. The only service most sites are ever offered for it is a skip bin.

The difference

A recovery job, not a rubbish job.

A skip is priced on volume and ends at a landfill gate. A recovery job starts by asking what each thing is actually made of, and where that material is worth something.

Hardwood beams and sleepers can be re-milled or reused. Steel and aluminium are commodities. Concrete, brick and rock are crushed into recycled aggregate at our own Moorabool site. Cable is granulated for its copper. Polystyrene is densified. What is genuinely finished gets reduced in volume before it goes anywhere near a cell — and you get told which was which.

Who this is for

Sites that generate material by operating.

Ports, terminals & marine

Decommissioned equipment, steel, timber, concrete and marine plant — often stored on hardstand for years because no one has offered a better answer than a bin.

Industrial estates & manufacturing

Line upgrades, obsolete machinery, packaging volumes, EPS and cable. Recurring streams that suit a scheduled arrangement rather than a one-off.

Facilities, property & infrastructure

Fit-out strips, plant replacement, multi-site clean-ups and end-of-project clearing, where someone needs one partner and one set of paperwork.

How it runs

Walk the site. Then price the work.

  1. A walk with someone who knows materials

    Not a sales call. Someone who can look at a stack and tell you whether it is worth something.

  2. A written snapshot

    What is on site, what each item is made of, and the recovery pathway for each — so you can take it to whoever signs.

  3. An agreed plan and sequence

    What moves first, what stays, access and lifting requirements, and who does what.

  4. The clear-out

    Our crews and trucks, or yours into our facility. Material separated at the point of pick-up wherever it is practical.

  5. A close-out by material

    What left the site, what it was, and where it went.

Site clearing runs on the group's own crews and trucks.
